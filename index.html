Between then and now

It’s our story, from a little further away than either of us could stand at the time.

I went back through all of it. The good parts, the parts I’m not proud of, and the long quiet in between. I’ve tried to tell it honestly, which means my mistakes are in here too, and they come first.

Read it whenever, at whatever pace.

What we had

About seventy-six thousand messages, and most of them are about nothing. Ki kheli. Pouchechis? Bari dhuklam. I used to think the big things held us together. Reading it back, it was the nothing.

You put it better than I could, this July: that our food habits, travel habits, sleeping habits, everything was somehow in sync. Dal chawal with chingri. Beef achar.

The things I keep finding in our chat: a Chikmagalur morning, mutton kosha from a Bong Eats video, bajar, and momo on the way back, your tripod, five kinds of bhaja, a night bus to Pondicherry, and good morning ❤️❤️❤️.

Tap any of them.

That November you wrote from Kolkata, walking by the Ganga with cha, missing our Chikmagalur morning. Much later you said a trip with just me somehow didn’t feel boring. Coming from someone who hates compliments, I’ve decided to count that as one.

The first time I made it, following a Bong Eats video. A year and a half later you said you still remember the taste. I’m not sure the cooking was that good. I think the evening was.

You told me in May that you missed going bajar together and eating momo, or anything at all, on the way back. Of everything you could have missed, that one. It’s the one I miss too.

It came with me to Tosh and Kutla in January 2025. “All thanks to your tripod,” I wrote. It’s still with me. It’s still yours.

February 2024: you wanted to keep my childhood tradition of five kinds of bhaja on my birthday. I don’t think anyone had ever made a plan that small, and that kind, for me.

Thursday night bus out, Sunday night bus back, a cheap place to stay. You planned the whole thing in one message, and it was exactly as good as you said it would be.

In 2024 we said good morning to each other 574 times, and sent about 3,100 hearts. Nobody counts these while they’re happening. I only counted now because I wanted to know what ordinary used to look like.

What the chat remembers

I counted a few things neither of us could have noticed while they were happening. None of it proves anything. It’s just what was there.

Words we caught from each other

Babah was always yours. You’ve said it 790 times. But in 2024 I said it 247 times too, without ever noticing I’d picked it up. Aladai was mine. This year you’ve said it 53 times, and I’ve said it 32. Somewhere along the way, we swapped.

Some words never crossed over at all. You called things sundor 256 times; I said it three times. I said cute instead, 112 times.

Our hour

The busiest hour of the entire chat was midnight to one. In 2024, almost a quarter of everything we said was said after eleven at night. It’s still our hour now.

Each spoke is one hour of the day, midnight at the top. Longer means we talked more then.

Under a minute

When we were talking, we were really talking. In 2024, the usual gap between your message and my reply was eighteen seconds; mine to yours, thirty. Out of more than twenty-four thousand replies that year, only eighteen times did one of us wait more than a day for the other.

The stretched letters

Babahhh. Niceeee. In 2024, about one in five of your messages had a letter stretched out like that, and one in six of mine. After January 2025 they nearly vanished. They’re slowly coming back.

One thing I didn’t expect: in those first weeks of January 2025, you were still stretching yours. I had already stopped. If one of us went flat first, it was me.

Four hundred and forty-nine calls

About seventy-eight hours in all, most of them ten minutes on the way somewhere. The longest call we ever had was three hours, on 19 January 2025, trying to hold on. Our last call was five days later.

Every call, January 2024 to January 2025. Height is how long it lasted.

Who laughed

In 2024 you laughed in text almost three times as often as I did. This year, I’ve nearly caught up. I’ve been learning.

Almost exactly even

In 2024 you started 170 of our conversations and I started 163. You said good morning first 152 times, I did 137 times. Nobody was chasing anybody. That’s what it looked like when it worked.

What we were really talking about

If you add it up, a surprising amount of us was food.

Paragraphs

In all of 2024, we sent each other 38 messages longer than a paragraph. In 2026 so far, 45. We only learned to say long things after it ended.

What went quiet

We didn’t fight much. I think that was part of the problem. This is the pattern as I see it now. I’ve put my side of each step first, because most of the time, my side started it.

A story about someone else

I’d bring up a friend’s relationship, a cousin’s wedding, someone’s surname, someone’s trip, and argue a side, as if it were just an idea.

You heard a verdict on you. On your freedom, your plans, the kind of life you wanted.

Why?

I asked for reasons. Lots of them. I genuinely thought that was what talking was.

Being asked to justify “I don’t want to” felt like being told your no wasn’t enough on its own.

Valid, invalid

I sorted your reasons out loud into ones I accepted and ones I didn’t.

You started feeling like the odd one. Like your thoughts only counted once someone approved them.

Okay, fine

I took your agreement as agreement, and moved on, relieved.

Sometimes you agreed just so the conversation would stop.

Kept inside

I told myself, and you, that we were on the same team, and believed it.

It stayed with you. Every time it came up again, it got a little heavier.

All at once

From my side it looked sudden. I panicked and pushed harder: talk now, call me, let me come.

From yours it was months old, and my pushing seemed to prove the fear right.

Neither of us built this on purpose. I fed it with questions; you fed it with silence. Mostly, I think, we were both trying not to hurt the other one. I called it a chain of inexpressive behaviour once, half joking. It’s still the most accurate thing I’ve said about us.

What I understand now

That I was never really fighting about marriage. Marriage was just the only picture I had of we stay. When you said not yet, I heard not ever, and I panicked in the most logical-sounding way I could.

That my family was my conversation to have, early and plainly, and I let it leak onto your shoulders instead. You told me that long before I did anything about it.

That you never owed me reasons. That space isn’t a verdict. That your independence was one of the first things I loved about you, so I don’t get to resent it when it applies to me.

And that I don’t think you broke us, and I don’t think I did alone either. I think two people who loved each other got scared in opposite directions. I moved closer; you moved away; and each move frightened the other more.

You said something at Bijoya last year that I’ve thought about more than almost anything: people don’t fall out of love, people create distance. I think we both did. Just differently.

If we ever chose to try again

This isn’t an ask. It’s just what I would bring, if you ever wanted it. Small things, because the small things were always what worked for us.

  • Your no is enough.When you say you don’t want something, I’ll say thik ache, we can wait, and mean it, without a follow-up question.
  • Marriage isn’t on the table.Not on a timeline, not in a debate, not through anyone else’s story. If it ever comes up, it’ll be because you brought it up.
  • My family is my conversation.I'll not dump every conversation as it is; no matter how much transparent we are, I have to be remembered that things can get conveyed very differently than how it actually is.
  • No more arguing through other people.If I have a feeling about us, I’ll say it about us.
  • I’ll ask before I come.Every time. Even for cha. Especially for cha.
  • Space means space.If you need quiet, I’ll tell you once what I’m feeling, “I miss you, I’m a little scared,” and then actually leave you the room.
  • Say it while it’s small.You told me fighting is important, for real. Let’s fight small and kind, on the day it happens, and then go get momo.This one is an invitation, not a condition. It’s my job to make it feel safe enough to be worth it.

I don’t know if we’ll ever try. I’ve made a kind of peace with not knowing. You said once that when your thoughts feel steady, you’d tell me you’re ready to meet. That’s still yours to say, whenever you want to, and it’s okay if you never do.

Whatever you decide, I’m glad it was you. Thank you for all of it, the seventy-six thousand messages and the years before the chat got deleted.
